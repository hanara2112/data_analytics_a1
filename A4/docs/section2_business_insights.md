# Section 2, Task 1: Business insights

1. Married customers (864) and those living together (580) account for 64.46% of all 2,240 customers. Household purchasing patterns are therefore useful when developing customer segments.

2. Ages 40–49 form the largest age group, with 676 customers, or 30.22% of the 2,237 customers with valid ages. Compare their spending with other age groups before prioritising them for campaigns.

3. The 30,000–39,999 income band contains 362 customers (16.16%). Its size makes it worth comparing average spending and response rates with adjacent income bands.

4. Total purchases and total spending have a Pearson correlation of 0.821. Purchase frequency is a useful indicator of customer spending, though it does not measure profitability.

5. Income and total spending have a correlation of 0.665. This rises to 0.820 when the eight flagged incomes are excluded, so income-based conclusions depend on the treatment of those records.

Counts and correlations were checked against the cleaned CSV. The Power BI correlation matrix describes the full dataset and does not recalculate when slicers change.
